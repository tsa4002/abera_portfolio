ABOUT THE PROJECT

Infinicine is an experimental video player that dynamically transitions between different movies based on overlapping dialogue. A user enters a word and is placed into a scene where that word is spoken. As the dialogue unfolds, the player finds a shared word in another movie and hops to that scene, creating a surreal, hybrid narrative from fragments of many films.

Inspiration

I stumbled upon Infinite Jukebox, a hackathon project that creates a never-ending song by hopping between similar-sounding beats. I loved visualizing the possible paths a song could take and wondered whether something similar could work for movies.

How it works

I sourced public-domain films across genres, then used FFmpeg to standardize resolution, audio levels and frame rates. Subtitle files were too imprecise for natural transitions, so I used Whisper for word-level timestamps, cleaned low-confidence and throwaway words, and built an index mapping each word to its occurrences across the films. During playback, the program selects a word from the current scene, finds an eligible occurrence in another movie, and jumps to it.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

ScrabbleSnake combines the letter tiles and point system of Scrabble with the collection mechanics of the arcade game Snake. Players move through an arena and collect the Scrabble tile letters in the order words are spelled to gain points.

Inspiration

At the risk of a cease and desist letter, I will confess ScrabbleSnake is a compound of the Hasbro game Scrabble and the popular arcade game Snake. I designed the tiles and point system with Scrabble in mind. The gameplay itself skews more towards the slither.io iteration of snake where drops can be collected that lengthen the snake's body in an expansive map.

How it works

ScrabbleSnake is a three mode browser game built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. A fixed-timestep game loop updates the snake's position, handles collisions, and spawns new letters. CSS animations drive visual feedback for events like shaking tiles, heart losses, and timer bar transitions. Sound effects reflect the feel of a physical board game, such as the crash of the tiles after the user loses their letters. I meshed this effect with chiptune sounds for the background music to bring in the energy of classic 8-bit arcade games.

Play the game!Tools: JS, CSS

ABOUT THE PROJECT

An interactive tool for discovering connections between two musical artists through overlapping collaborations

Inspiration

I was inspired to make Six Degrees of Sound while playing the six-degrees-of-separation parlor game with friends. We quickly connected ourselves to religious figures, presidents and celebrities, and the conversation turned to graph theory. I wondered whether a similar network could be drawn within music using overlapping collaborations.

How it works

After a user selects two artists, a bidirectional breadth-first search traverses the MusicBrainz dataset to find a connection. Each artist is a node, and an edge connects artists who appear together on a recording. Album covers and track previews are pulled from Apple Music as each intermediary link is displayed. I filtered the community-maintained MusicBrainz data to remove non-musical entries, unofficial remixes and recordings unaffiliated with a label, then optimized the resulting graph for pathfinding.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

An interactive augmented reality experience that uses hand tracking to let people manipulate 3D protein structures in space. It turns abstract molecular arrangements into something more intuitive for teaching, learning and engaging with structural biology.

Inspiration

As an undergraduate, I joined a biochemistry research lab studying how a protein transports molecules in and out of a cell. One principle came up repeatedly: protein form determines protein function. Understanding complex protein structures through 2D projections and static models can be challenging, so I wanted to make their shapes easier to explore in three dimensions.

How it works

I downloaded protein structures from the Protein Data Bank and customized the models in ChimeraX to highlight structural features. I exported them as GLB files into Lens Studio and programmed hand tracking in JavaScript so users could rotate and scale the proteins. A facial-gesture trigger reveals protein names and measured finger vectors when users point to each name.

Tools: AR, Javascript

After years of futilely begging for a pet, my brother decided it was better to ask for forgiveness than permission and smuggled in a small tuxedo kitten named Po. My parents, incredulous at his gall, tried to weed out any co-conspirators but it was truly a unilateral move. I admitted my admiration for the tactic but confessed I would’ve adopted a dog.

Worried the house was on track to resemble a zoo, my parents tried to use the unglamorous side of pet ownership as a deterrent. They made him tally up the costs of food, toys, pet insurance, grooming, and any incidental expense. He had to map out a training plan, research local vets, and draft out a cleaning schedule. Seeing the full weight of details spelled out, I joined my parents in swearing off any responsibility that could spawn from her.

My brother dutifully nodded his head and hid his widening grin, aware that his gambit had worked.

Po was running a gambit of her own, rapidly working her way into our hearts with tiny chirps and the chaotic grace of darting between our legs at every turn. No bigger than the palm of my hand, she explored her new home with an oversized curiosity. She loved to perch on top of my shoulders and coil up on my bed. It wasn’t long before I started buying her toys, seeing the previous responsibilities not as things I had to do but things I would want to do.

We grew up but she didn’t change much in size. She would fall into bouts of mysterious illness that anxious vet visits failed to resolve, leaving symptom management as the only viable course of action. Regardless, her playful spirit never diminished. Every time I returned home from college, she would bolt down to scold me with indignant chirps, as if demanding to know where I’d been.

Then, just as quickly, she’d settle back into old habits, clambering up and draping herself across my shoulder to reclaim her favorite post.

When a digital art assignment called for a self-portrait, it only felt natural to include Po. I took a photo of her overlooking from her usual spot on my shoulder and adjusted the saturation, drew out the negative space to suggest an emerging figure, and added a soft halo above her head as a reference to the shoulder angels trope common in animation.

Two days later, I received a late-night call from my sister. Her voice cracked as she told me Po had an emergency and was rushed to the vet. They diagnosed her with feline infectious peritonitis and recommended euthanasia. Through tears, I said goodbye over a video call as Po lay heavily sedated on a table, her once energetic frame now motionless.

In the days after Po passed, I returned to the self-portrait. What had started as Po’s lighthearted inclusion now carried deeper weight. I adjusted the contrast and brightened the halo’s glow. Above her, I added stained glass arches that gently descended, as if reaching to lift her upward.

Tools: Illustrator

ABOUT THE PROJECT

Cacophony turns dialogue from multiple films into an interactive audiovisual collage. A user enters a word and a grid of clips plays simultaneously until the different scenes sync on that word, briefly forming a chaotic chorus before falling back into disarray.

Inspiration

While building Infinicine, I kept thinking about other ways to repurpose the extracted movie clips. Cacophony grew from that question: instead of moving between films, what if several clips played at once and converged on the same spoken word?

How it works

Cacophony uses Infinicine’s word-level timestamps and lookup index. When a user enters a word, a Python script selects up to eight occurrences, applies adjustable lead-in and lead-out buffers, and uses FFmpeg to cut the clips. On the front end, a 3×3 CSS grid reserves the center cell for the input. JavaScript places the clips around it, preloads them and starts synchronized playback with audio. Drag-and-drop handlers let users rearrange the cells and immediately resynchronize playback.

Tools: Python, Bash, JS
